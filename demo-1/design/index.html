Why it looks like this

The best-regarded paywalls share one shape: a single column — visible close button and Restore at the top, one benefit headline, a few scannable benefits, 2–3 plan cards with one clearly highlighted plan, and a sticky full-width call-to-action that repeats the selected plan and price (RevenueCat; Nami; Science of Apps' teardown of 100 paywalls from 66 apps).

Premium comes from restraint, not decoration: one accent colour for action, generous whitespace, and the same type and corner radii as the rest of the app. Here, dark plum is the field and gold is reserved — the Ultimate border, the selected-card outline, and the buy button only. If everything is gold, nothing is. The rising bubbles stay in the background, exactly as locked for the app — the cards themselves stay clean.

  • Three plans, not five. 2–3 paid plans is the consensus; three-plan paywalls drive stronger lifetime value than single-plan ones via anchoring (Adapty, 16,000+ apps). The old five-card stack is gone — Free is a banner, and 100 vs 300 lives inside the Checks card.
  • The toggle sits inside the card it changes, keeps the same feature list in both states, and updates the sticky button's price instantly. Nothing hides behind it.
  • Honest anchoring. 300 checks at $6.99 is ≈2.3¢ a check vs ≈5.0¢ at 100 — about 53% cheaper per check, 3× the checks for $2 more. Both prices are real and purchasable today, which is the test for any anchor: could a customer have paid that price yesterday?
  • Ultimate at $14.99 — more, not cheaper. The pitch is capacity, told straight: both plans plus more — 500 checks and 50 grade runs for $3.01 more than buying Checks 300 + Grader apart ($11.98). No “bundle saving” claim, because the numbers don't say that.
  • Split rule, printed on the cards. Plan checks refresh monthly and don't roll over; free checks expire each month; checks bought outright never expire. The line sits on the Checks and Ultimate cards, not buried in terms.
  • The screen may scroll; the buy button may not leave. 59–76% of top paywalls scroll — the call-to-action and disclosure stay pinned instead.
  • No gimmicks. Countdown timers appear on under 1.4% of top paywalls and progress bars under 0.2% (RevenueCat). There are none here — no fake scarcity, no struck-through price that was never charged, no invented user counts.

Google Pay — first, big, and honest about how it works

On Android, in-app digital goods must go through Google Play Billing — there is no separate Google Pay button to add to this screen in the standard path, and drawing a fake one would promise a checkout the app can't offer. The no-typing experience you want is delivered by Play Billing itself: tap the gold button, Google Play shows its own purchase sheet, and you confirm with the payment method already saved to your Google Account. So the design makes the gold button the largest, highest-contrast element on the screen, puts the “no card details to enter here” reassurance right under it in readable type, settles every choice (plan + toggle) before the tap, and puts nothing — no form, no account wall, no upsell — between the tap and Google's sheet. Copy says “Confirm in Google Play,” never “1-tap,” because Play may ask for biometric/password verification and newer Play versions use a “Slide to buy” gesture. Cancelling in the sheet returns here quietly, selection intact — no guilt copy, no surprise discount pop-up.

Sign in with Google — same idea, our colours

The white Google button sits under the plans in the app's own plum-and-gold frame: one tap, the account you already use, and your plans follow you to a new phone. It's offered, never required — research and Play policy both warn against an account wall between the user and payment, so signing in is a convenience here, not a gate.

Two decisions — locked in

1 · Ultimate is $14.99, pitched on more. Checks 300 ($6.99) + Grader ($4.99) bought separately = $11.98/mo for 300 checks + 20 grade runs. Ultimate at $14.99/mo is $3.01 more for 500 checks + 50 grade runs — both plans, plus more. That's the whole pitch, printed on the card. It never claims to be a saving or “best value,” because against the separate total it isn't one — it's more allowance for a little more money.

2 · The split rule for rollover. Locked: plan checks refresh monthly — the 100/300 (and Ultimate's 500) are a monthly allowance and unused plan checks don't roll over; free checks expire each month; purchased checks never expire — if someone buys checks outright, they're theirs until used. That keeps Checks a genuine recurring monthly allowance, as Play requires for a subscription, and puts the term in plain words on the card itself instead of hiding it.

Trust, printed on the screen — not behind a link

  • Close (✕) visible from the first second, top-left; Restore Purchases top-right and again in the footer. 97 of 100 top paywalls were dismissible — a delayed or hidden close is a dark pattern.
  • Full disclosure under the button: renews automatically at the shown price, cancel anytime in Google Play → Subscriptions, access kept until the end of the paid month. Play requires all of this readable with no extra tap.
  • Rollover printed where you buy: plan checks refresh monthly, free checks expire, purchased checks never expire — stated on the Checks and Ultimate cards, not only in terms.
  • Free framed positively: the banner states the real free allowance and that the app works without paying — Play lists hiding that as a violation.
  • No social proof appears on this screen, because there is no verified rating or review to quote yet. Real or none.
  • Prices come from Play, not hard-coded strings, so they stay localised and match Play Console — the dollar figures in this mockup are the locked ones from the brief.

Do

  • Single column; headline → benefits → cards → sticky button
  • One highlighted plan — badge, border and per-check math all on the same card
  • Billed price biggest; per-check math second
  • Sticky button repeats plan + price and updates live
  • Button → Play sheet immediately; saved Google method does the rest

Don't

  • Countdowns, “only X left,” invented struck-through prices
  • A fake Google Pay button or an in-app card form
  • “Most popular” or “best value” claims the numbers don't back
  • Terms only behind a link, or in grey micro-type
  • Extra screens between the tap and Google's sheet

The menu — a slide-out drawer, built to the researched spec

The per-screen research (sources in the guide section below) fixes the split the way Discord's engineers landed on it: the bottom bar is the primary navigation, the drawer is secondary — and no destination appears in both. The drawer holds only the eight long-tail doors: Grader, How checks work, Rewards, Leaderboard, Invite & earn, Guide, Talk to my agent, Feedback. Home, Binders, Games and Check live in the bar and nowhere else.

  • Width: min(84% of screen, 320pt), 16pt rounded trailing corners, plum-black scrim at ~45% — a tappable strip of the app always stays visible. (Drawer-width sources disagree — 280dp classic cap vs 360dp Material 3 token — so this is a reconciled rule inside all three ranges, not one canonical number.)
  • Exactly two ways in: the labelled Menu slot in the bottom navigation, and a left-edge swipe on root screens. No top hamburger as well — two triggers is the cap, and the bottom slot is the reachable one. Edge-swipe is off inside the camera, games and grader, where it would fight the screen's own gestures.
  • Four ways closed: tap the scrim, drag it back left, system Back, or the ✕. Picking a destination closes the drawer first, then navigates. Slide is 250–300ms ease-out; Reduce Motion swaps it for a short fade.
  • Header is the account door: avatar, name and the two balance chips (coins · checks left) are display-only — tapping the header itself opens Profile & Settings. No Pro pill or upsell lives in the header, and at most one dot badge ever appears in the drawer (Rewards, only when a reward is genuinely unclaimed).
  • Honest caveat from the same research: current platform guidance is cooling on drawers — Material 3 Expressive no longer recommends them, and Reddit removed its side profile drawer in Dec 2025. The owner's drawer still works because it is kept secondary: it must never swallow a primary destination later.

What carries to every screen

  • Bubbles are background dressing only — they rise behind the screen; cards and buttons stay clean on top.
  • Gold is the action colour — gradient on the one main button per screen, selected states, nothing else competing.
  • One column, generous space — if a screen needs a scroll to find its main button, something comes out.
  • Same corners, same type — Nunito, rounded, identical radii from screen to screen.

What got taken out

  • The top drop-down menu — gone. Replaced by a slide-out side menu (the Menu slot in the bottom navigation, or an edge swipe) plus the five-slot bottom navigation.
  • “Upgrade” and “Tells” as tabs — gone from the bar. Upgrade becomes a quiet “Pro” inside Profile & Settings and at real feature gates; Tells is renamed “How checks work” — jargon may be taught inside the page, never required on a tab.
  • The binder header art — binders open on the cards themselves, not a wall of pictures.
  • Sign-in walls — Google leads, email and phone follow quietly, and guests can look around first.
  • Anything decorative that doesn't act — every button on these screens does its job when tapped.
The guide · one guided section per screen

Every screen, guided one by one

This is the finished per-screen design research (read 2026-11-14), applied: for each screen the app actually has, the winning layout from the best comparable apps, the patterns that keep it calm and premium, and what to avoid — with a live phone in the same plum-and-gold language beside each guide. The identity never changes: deep plum, rising bubbles behind the hero zone only, Nunito, and one gold element per screen, maximum — always that screen's single primary step.

Global rules the research sets for every screen: an 8pt spacing grid (16pt side margins, 24pt between sections, 12pt inside cards); one card style app-wide (16–20pt radius, flat plum fill, no border-plus-shadow stacking); titles ≤3 words, section headers ≤4, buttons 1–3 words, explanations ≤2 lines before a fold; touch targets ≥44pt iOS / 48dp Android; at most two sticky elements per screen; and the bubbles fade to flat by the first content card and pause under Reduce Motion. Hierarchy comes from scale, spacing and placement — only 2–3 type sizes per screen (NN/g, nngroup.com).

Binders — the guided layout

Comparables: Dex (binder display mode + insight cards), Collectr (portfolio grid + filters), Ludex/CollX-style portfolio lists.

  • Slim sticky header, ~96–110pt, collapsing on scroll: binder name (tap = switcher sheet) + view toggle; then one summary line only — “128 cards · $1,240 · 3 need a second look”. Dex leads with a row of insight cards; here the full insights (verdict split, rarity breakdown, set rings) live on a separate Insights view reached from that line, not stacked on the binder.
  • Search + two chips only — Sort, Filter. Dex's own release notes show the failure and the fix: binder controls “crowded the filter row” until Sort and Types each collapsed into a single chip that opens a sheet. Chips open sheets; never a wrapping row of six filters.
  • Grid: 3 columns, photo-first. The photo fills the tile (cards are portrait — use the ratio), exactly two lines under it (name, price), and the verdict is a small pill overlaid top-left with colour + word, never icon-only — an icon-only verdict is an honesty failure for colour-blind users. Inconclusive cards get a findable treatment so “needs a second look” can be scanned for.
  • Binder view: paged 9-pocket pages (3×3), swipe to turn — Dex's Binder Display Mode offers 4 / 9 / 12 / 16 pockets with 9 as default, saved per collection (dextcg.com). Empty pockets render as quiet empty sleeves.
  • Set completion is one quiet row — thin bar + “214 / 286 · 75%”, shown when a set filter is active. Never a ring + bar + badge all three.
  • Card detail: your own front/back photos first (swipe between them), verdict + date checked, price with source + date, grader entry point, and binder move/remove in an overflow menu — not buttons stacked on the photo.

Why it feels premium

Photo-first, metadata-second — the grid reads as a binder, not a spreadsheet. Collectr's two-column tiles carry image, name, set, rarity, grade, quantity, price and change — investor density this app drops to name + price + verdict pill. One sticky slim header, a regular (never staggered) grid, and no floating scan button over the collection: scanning is the centre tab.

Avoid: filter-chip sprawl; per-tile red/green price-change arrows (stock-ticker noise); multi-select checkboxes visible by default (Dex disables multi-select in Binder mode because it fights the binder illusion — long-press enters select mode instead); and never mix marketplace cards into “my binder” — it holds the user's own checked cards only.

The cautionary tale

PictureThis is the warning: never bury the verdict under price or an upsell, never paywall or blur the verdict or evidence behind Pro, and no upgrade prompt before the result is readable. Pro can gate history depth and exports — never the answer to “is it real?”. No countdowns or “credits running out” pressure on this screen, no rating prompt right after subscribing, no confetti for genuine and no shaming for counterfeit — the tone stays lab-calm in both directions.

Grader — the guided layout

Comparables: Typeform / Duolingo / Monzo one-question-per-screen wizards; grading-company criteria (PSA/CGC/BGS/SGC all grade centering, corners, edges, surface).

  • Five steps, one question per screen, each fitting without scrolling. Persistent slim top bar: ✕ (exit — progress is saved), “Step 2 of 5” in words + a thin bar, nothing else. Any flow of 3+ steps shows honest progress, a back path on every step, and answers that persist through back/forward. The primary button is pinned at the bottom, full-width.
  • The sequence: S1 Photos (front + back — if launched from a check, the photos carry over and the screen says so) · S2 Corners · S3 Edges & surface (with a tilt tip: surface flaws “can be invisible until you tilt the card under a light”) · S4 Centering — interactive, the app overlays a centering guide on your front photo; it's the most measurable criterion and the most common reason a clean card misses a 10, and it feels like a lab instrument, not a quiz · S5 Anything else? (creases, writing, stains — multi-select + “None of these”).
  • Step titles are questions in the user's words, not database labels, and help text stays ≤20 words per screen — the photo examples inside the option cards do the teaching.
  • Estimate screen: one row per company — PSA · CGC · BGS · SGC — each an estimated range in large type plus the limiting factor in one line. Avoid: one long scrolling form with every condition question (the exact pattern one-question flows beat); questions whose answers can't change the estimate (cut the step); a progress bar that stalls at 90% or invents extra steps; presenting the estimate as a guarantee or a single false-precise number; and never gating the estimate behind Pro after the user answered five steps — if grading depth is Pro, say so on the door, before Step 1.

  • Leaderboard: scope toggle Friends / This week (never a global all-time board by default), your row pinned, and the list centred on you — research is explicit that boards should show “users who are slightly ahead of them, not just the top 0.1%”, that friend boards beat global ones, and that weekly boards give everyone a fresh start (trophy.so). Duolingo's leagues group ~30 similar-level users per week. The metric is effort (checks this week) — a coin-purchase metric on a leaderboard is a casino pattern.
  • Invite & earn: the screen must state, without tapping anything, what you get, what your friend gets, what they must do, and when the reward lands (screensdesign.com). One primary button — Share my invite — opening the native share sheet with an editable message in the user's voice and the code inside the link. Below: copyable code, the 3-step sequence, invite statuses in words, and the limits in plain text. Surface the ask after a success moment (a genuine verdict, a grader result), never as an interstitial at signup.
  • Store-policy flag: Apple reviewers have objected to rewarding the invited person merely for downloading or registering; the safer construction — used here — is that both rewards fire on an in-app action, the friend's first completed check (warplink.app).
  • Feedback timing: rewards land visibly at the moment of earning — the one quiet “+5 coins” line on the Result screen — because delayed, invisible rewards kill repeat behaviour. One line, no coin-shower animation.

The casino line, explicitly

No demotion or shame mechanics (Duolingo demotes its bottom five — do not copy it), no streak-loss threats, no expiring coins without a long, plainly-stated window, no mystery or variable rewards, no spin wheels, no near-miss copy, no paid leaderboard boosts, no public rank without opt-in. And don't over-reward the core loop: heavy extrinsic rewards can crowd out the intrinsic reason to check a card — coins stay small-denomination garnish, never the reason the verdict screen exists.

Navigation — the two dedicated build guides

Bottom navigation (the bar). Apple HIG allows 3–5 tabs on iPhone and Material 3 serves 3–5 destinations; NN/g's data (179 participants) found hidden navigation cut discoverability by over 20% and mobile tasks ran 15% slower than combo navigation, which was used 86% of the time on mobile vs 57% for hidden-only. Six slots at 390pt wide leaves ~65pt per slot — labels truncate below comfortable targets. So the bar is 5 visual slots, only 3 of them destination tabs: Home · Binders · [Check — raised centre] · Games · Menu. “Upgrade” leaves the bar entirely (a tab bar is for navigation, not actions — and a permanent sales slot is a standing pressure trick); it becomes “Pro” inside Profile & Settings and at genuine feature gates, never gold, never badged. “Tells” is renamed “How checks work” (subtitle: “The tells we look for”; on the Result screen it's linked as “Why this verdict?”).

The raised centre Check is the category's proven shape: TikTok's and YouTube's centre Create buttons taught it to everyone, and Collectr — per a third-party template description, treated as indicative — puts its scanner in the centre of a five-slot bar (Portfolio, Search, Scan, Social, Shop). Spec: 56–60pt gold circle raised 12–16pt above the bar, “Check” label beneath, full centre slot as the tap target, press = slight sink, never disabled, never badged, never pulsing. Apple HIG says tab bars are for navigation, not actions — this is a deliberate, widely-shipped exception, justified because Check is the app's single gold primary action everywhere and is visually separated from the tabs so it never reads as a fourth tab. Never add a second action to the bar.

Active states & badges: active tab = filled icon + full-strength label; inactive ≈55–60% opacity; labels always shown on all five slots; re-tapping the active tab scrolls it to top; each tab keeps its own scroll position. Badges are rationed to near-zero: a dot only, never a count, allowed on Games only when today's pull is genuinely unclaimed — never on Menu, never red.

The drawer is specified in the callout under the home phone above: modal, min(84%, 320pt), eight rows in three groups, tappable identity header with display-only balances, zero duplicated doors. Discord's engineers documented why: running a drawer and bottom tabs as two primary systems “violated Material Design guidelines… made the information hierarchy ambiguous” — the fix was exactly this split (discord.com). NN/g's lesson is not “never hide anything” — it's “never hide the primary destinations”; this design hides only weekly-or-less destinations.

Home (guide applied to the phone above): Collectr's home is a portfolio — one live total value, then lists (selfmadenewbie.com) — adapted as one wide collection card (value + Cards checked · Fakes caught · Needs a second look, the third stat a working door into filtered Binders), the gold Check directly under it, recent checks, then two equal half-width doors. No chart on Home v1 and no red/green gain-loss framing — that makes a collection feel like a casino ticker. Avoid the TCGplayer pattern (scanner buried in a store, marketplace noise above the collection) and the PictureThis pattern (articles, tips and upgrade prompts mixed into home). Exactly three visual weights on the first screen: collection card, gold button, everything else quiet.

Sign-in (guide applied to the phone above): Google's branding guidelines require the Google button be at least as prominent as any other third-party option, with the standard multicolour G and an approved verb — “Continue” covers new and returning users without a fork (developers.google.com). If any third-party login is offered, Sign in with Apple must be an equivalent option at the same size and weight (Apple Guideline 4.8). The magic link email sends both a link and a short code — a link only works smoothly on the device that reads the email; a code can be typed anywhere. Ask late: first check as guest, sign-in at save-to-binder or the second check. No password field anywhere.

Purchase (guide §6b, brief pass): reached only from a Pro door or a feature gate — never the app's first screen, never auto-presented after a check. 2–3 benefit rows tied to real features, plan cards with the full billed amount at least as prominent as any per-month maths, trial and renewal terms in plain words beside the CTA, Restore Purchases present, a visible “Not now” with an equal tap target and neutral wording (never confirmshaming), and “Cancel anytime — as easy as signing up.” RevenueCat's hard-paywall guidance reduces to the same skeleton (revenuecat.com) — this app uses a soft, dismissible version. The locked purchase phone at the top of this page already follows it.

Never — the consolidated brand-conflict list

  • Countdown timers / fake urgency anywhere (Games pull, paywall, invite) — the FTC names fake urgency as a dark pattern. State reset times as text instead.
  • Paywall before or over the verdict — verdict + evidence are always free.
  • Confirmshaming decline copy — neutral “Not now”, equal tap target.
  • Hidden renewal price, per-month maths larger than the billed total, hard-to-cancel mazes, delayed or hidden paywall close buttons.
  • Always-on badges and a badged Menu — one dot, Games only, genuinely unclaimed only.
  • False-precision confidence (“97.3% authentic”) — words + evidence rows instead.
  • Demotion leagues, streak threats, mystery rewards, and a permanent “Upgrade” tab or upsell in the drawer header.
  • Rewarding a friend's install — store-risk; rewards fire on the first completed check.
