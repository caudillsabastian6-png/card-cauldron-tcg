Grade It or
Keep It Raw?

Grading is sold like a lottery ticket and priced like a tax. Before you ship a card off for months, run the real numbers: the grading fee, shipping both ways, and what a 10, a 9, or anything lower actually adds over selling the card raw. Most of the time, the honest answer is the boring one.

Pick both and we'll show the all-in cost, the break-even sale you'd need, and our verdict. Everything is a range — anybody who gives you one exact number is selling something.

01 — THE REAL BILLS

The fee is not the bill

The per-card fee is the headline. The bill is the fee plus shipping your cards to the grader, plus return shipping back — and return shipping is charged per order, which is why grading one card alone is the most expensive way to do it. Turnaround times below are the graders' published estimates — not guarantees; both companies say so on their own pages.

Shipping, PSA: you pay to ship your cards in (tracked/insured carrier, commonly about $10–$30 for a small order), and PSA charges return shipping per order at checkout based on order value — recent guides put a small order's return leg around $20–$40. PSA's services page does not print one flat return rate, so check the grader's site for the current fee at checkout before you count on a number. If your card grades above your tier's value cap, PSA can move it up a tier and bill the difference.

02 — THE UPLIFT, HONESTLY

Why most cards stay raw

The whole bet lives or dies on one grade point. Here's what sold-price data — not listing prices, not hope — says about the gap.

~2× – 5×

What a 10 can add (modern cards)

Modern Pokémon cards that land a top grade commonly sell for roughly 2×–5× the same card raw — with plenty landing below that range, and flagship chase cards sometimes far above it. It is a range, not a rate. (Pokemon Price Tracker, 2026 raw-vs-graded analysis)

30% – 50%

What a 9 is worth vs. its own 10

A 9 typically sells for only 30%–50% of the same card's 10 price. In one study of 126 eBay sales of a single chase card, raw copies sold for almost the same as the graded 9s — the fee bought a plastic case, not a premium. (Pokemon Price Tracker 2026; Herrhana, 126 eBay sales, Jan–Jun 2026)

~9 in 10

Modern 9s that lose money

Across liquid markets, a modern (2022+) card that comes back a 9 sells for less than its raw price plus the grading fee roughly 90% of the time — and 8s and below often sell at or under raw. The value isn't in grading. It's in landing the 10. (TCGInvest market analysis, 1,959 liquid graded markets)

One more cost nobody puts on the poster: when you sell, marketplace fees (eBay's trading-card fee is about 13.25% of the total sale, per 2026 cost breakdowns) come off the graded sale too. The break-evens on this page are grading costs only — your card has to clear those and selling fees to actually come out ahead. Grading for your own shelf — protection, authentication, a card you love in a slab — is a perfectly good reason. Just don't let anybody call it an investment case when the math above says otherwise.
